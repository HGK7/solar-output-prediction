# Solar Energy Fundamentals

## What Is Solar Irradiance?

Solar irradiance is the power per unit area received from the Sun in the form of electromagnetic radiation, measured in watts per square metre (W/m²) or kilowatt-hours per square metre per day (kWh/m²/day). It is the primary input that determines how much electricity a photovoltaic (PV) system can generate.

Global Horizontal Irradiance (GHI) is the total amount of shortwave radiation received from above by a horizontal surface on the ground. It includes both Direct Normal Irradiance (DNI) and Diffuse Horizontal Irradiance (DHI).

The "All Sky Surface Shortwave Downward Irradiance" (ALLSKY_SFC_SW_DWN) reported by the NASA POWER dataset represents the total solar radiation reaching the Earth's surface under actual sky conditions, including the effects of clouds, aerosols, and atmospheric gases.

## Clear Sky Irradiance

Clear Sky Irradiance (CLRSKY_SFC_SW_DWN) represents the solar radiation that would reach the surface if there were no clouds. The ratio of actual irradiance to clear-sky irradiance indicates the cloud impact on solar energy production. A ratio close to 1.0 means nearly cloud-free conditions.

## How Solar Panels Convert Sunlight

Photovoltaic cells convert sunlight into electricity through the photovoltaic effect. When photons strike the semiconductor material (typically silicon), they knock electrons free from their atoms, creating an electric current. The efficiency of this conversion depends on the cell technology, temperature, and the spectrum of incoming light.

## Units and Measurement

- **W/m²**: Instantaneous power density (watts per square metre)
- **kWh/m²/day**: Daily energy density — the total energy received per square metre over a day
- A typical sunny location receives 4–7 kWh/m²/day of GHI
- Peak sun hours (PSH) is a simplified way to express daily irradiance: 5 kWh/m²/day ≈ 5 peak sun hours

## The Solar Resource in India

India receives abundant solar radiation, with most regions getting 4–7 kWh/m²/day. The western desert regions of Rajasthan and Gujarat receive the highest irradiance levels, making them ideal for large-scale solar installations. The annual average GHI across India ranges from approximately 1,600 to 2,200 kWh/m²/year.
