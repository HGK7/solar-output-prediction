# Bhadla Solar Park — Reference Information

## Overview

Bhadla Solar Park is the world's largest solar park, located in the Thar Desert of Rajasthan, India. It is situated near the village of Bhadla in Jodhpur district, at approximately 27.54°N latitude and 71.92°E longitude.

## Key Facts

- **Total capacity**: 2,245 MW (as of 2024)
- **Area**: Approximately 14,000 acres (5,700 hectares)
- **Location**: Bhadla, Jodhpur district, Rajasthan, India
- **Coordinates**: 27°32′23″N, 71°54′55″E
- **Elevation**: ~220 metres above sea level
- **Commissioned**: Phased commissioning from 2015 to 2020
- **Operator**: Various IPPs including NTPC, Adani, SB Energy (SoftBank)

## Climate Conditions

The Bhadla region has an arid desert climate (BWh in the Köppen classification), which is highly favourable for solar energy:

- **Annual average temperature**: 25–27°C
- **Summer peak temperatures**: Up to 48°C
- **Winter minimum temperatures**: 2–5°C
- **Annual rainfall**: 100–300 mm, concentrated in July–September monsoon
- **Average relative humidity**: 30–45% (low, except during monsoon)
- **Average wind speed**: 2–4 m/s
- **Annual GHI**: 2,000–2,100 kWh/m²/year (among highest in India)
- **Sunshine hours**: 300+ clear sunny days per year

## Solar Resource

Bhadla receives some of the highest solar radiation in India:

- **Peak daily irradiance**: 6.5–7.5 kWh/m²/day (May–June)
- **Minimum daily irradiance**: 3.5–4.5 kWh/m²/day (December–January)
- **Annual average**: ~5.5 kWh/m²/day
- **Capacity Utilization Factor (CUF)**: 22–24% for fixed-tilt systems

## Challenges

Despite excellent solar resources, Bhadla faces several challenges:

1. **Extreme heat**: Panel temperatures can exceed 70°C, causing 10–15% efficiency loss
2. **Dust and sand**: Frequent sandstorms require regular panel cleaning; soiling losses can reach 5–8% without cleaning
3. **Grid connectivity**: Remote location requires long transmission lines
4. **Water scarcity**: Limited water for panel cleaning in the desert environment
5. **Land temperature**: Ground temperatures can exceed 55°C, affecting inverter and cable performance

## Data Source

The prediction models in this system use data from the NASA POWER (Prediction of Worldwide Energy Resources) API, which provides satellite-derived meteorological data specifically designed for renewable energy applications. The data covers parameters including:

- ALLSKY_SFC_SW_DWN: All-Sky Surface Shortwave Downward Irradiance
- T2M: Temperature at 2 metres
- RH2M: Relative Humidity at 2 metres
- WS2M: Wind Speed at 2 metres
- CLRSKY_SFC_SW_DWN: Clear Sky Surface Shortwave Downward Irradiance
