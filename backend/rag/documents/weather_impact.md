# Weather Impact on Solar Energy Output

## Temperature Effects

Temperature has a counter-intuitive effect on solar panel output. While sunny days are often hot, higher temperatures actually reduce the efficiency of photovoltaic cells. For crystalline silicon panels, efficiency drops by approximately 0.3–0.5% per degree Celsius above 25°C (the Standard Test Condition temperature).

This means that a panel rated at 300W under STC may only produce 285W when the cell temperature reaches 55°C. In hot desert environments like Rajasthan, cell temperatures can exceed 70°C, causing significant performance derating.

Conversely, cold but sunny conditions (common in high-altitude or winter scenarios) can actually boost output above nameplate ratings.

## Humidity Effects

High humidity affects solar output through multiple mechanisms:

1. **Atmospheric absorption**: Water vapour absorbs certain wavelengths of sunlight, reducing the total irradiance reaching panels.
2. **Scattering**: Humid air scatters sunlight more, increasing diffuse radiation relative to direct radiation.
3. **Soiling**: Humid conditions promote dust accumulation and biological growth on panel surfaces.
4. **Condensation**: Morning dew can temporarily reduce output until panels dry.

In the Rajasthan region, humidity is generally low (30–50% annual average), which is favourable for solar energy production. During the monsoon season (July–September), humidity rises significantly, coinciding with reduced solar output due to cloud cover.

## Wind Speed Effects

Wind has a primarily positive effect on solar panel performance:

1. **Cooling**: Wind helps dissipate heat from panel surfaces, reducing cell temperature and improving efficiency.
2. **Dust removal**: Moderate winds can help keep panels cleaner.

However, extreme winds (above 25 m/s) can cause structural damage to mounting systems. Typical wind speeds at Bhadla Solar Park range from 1.5 to 5 m/s, which provides beneficial cooling without structural risk.

## Cloud Cover and Clear Sky Index

The most significant weather factor affecting solar output is cloud cover. The Clear Sky Index (ratio of actual to clear-sky irradiance) directly quantifies this impact:

- **Clear sky (index > 0.9)**: Nearly full solar potential realized
- **Partly cloudy (0.5–0.9)**: Moderate reduction; intermittent cloud shadows cause variability
- **Overcast (< 0.5)**: Significant reduction; output comes primarily from diffuse radiation
- **Heavy overcast (< 0.2)**: Very low output; only diffuse light available

## Seasonal Patterns

At Bhadla Solar Park (latitude 27.5°N), seasonal patterns include:

- **Summer (Apr–Jun)**: Highest irradiance (6–7 kWh/m²/day) but very high temperatures reduce efficiency
- **Monsoon (Jul–Sep)**: Lowest irradiance due to cloud cover and humidity; output drops 30–50%
- **Post-monsoon (Oct–Nov)**: Good irradiance with moderate temperatures — often best net output
- **Winter (Dec–Mar)**: Lower sun angle reduces irradiance but cool temperatures improve panel efficiency
